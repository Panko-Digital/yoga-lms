Tadasana Alignment

Tadasana, often called Mountain Pose, is the foundational Asana from which all standing postures originate. In this lesson we explore how correct alignment in Tadasana sets the stage for every Vinyasa sequence you will teach.

Begin by grounding through the feet, engaging Bandha to stabilise the pelvis, and finding your Drishti — a soft, steady gaze point that anchors your awareness. Notice how the breath naturally deepens when the spine is long and the shoulders are relaxed.

As you hold the pose, bring attention to Pranayama: slow, rhythmic breathing that connects movement to intention. This is the essence of a mindful practice. We also have a Sanskrit glossary.

Mudras & Energy Flow

Mudras are symbolic hand gestures used during meditation and Pranayama to direct the flow of energy through the body. In this section we look at three Mudras commonly paired with seated Asana practice.

Chin Mudra (consciousness seal) is formed by touching the tip of the index finger to the thumb while the remaining fingers extend softly. It is often used during Pranayama exercises to enhance focus and calm the mind.

Closing & Namaste

Every class ends with a moment of stillness. Guide your students back to Tadasana, hands at heart centre, and close with Namaste — a recognition of the shared experience of practice.

Remind students that the alignment principles explored today — Bandha, Drishti, and conscious Pranayama — carry beyond the mat into daily movement. Encourage them to notice their posture throughout the day as an extension of their Vinyasa practice.
